# Omar Achraf – 10 Minute Interview Narrative for xAI

# Introduction

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me.

I am a Computer Engineer from Brazil with a career spanning software, embedded systems, telecommunications, UAVs, autonomy, simulation and system integration.

What connects most of my work is not a specific technology, but complex systems engineering.

---

# Early Technical Background

Since childhood I have been involved with electronics, mechanics, fabrication, model aircraft and technical projects.

Those hobbies evolved into a lifelong interest in understanding how systems work, how they fail and how they can be improved.

---

# Computer Engineering and Graduate Research

My Computer Engineering education provided a strong foundation in computing, electronics, automation, control systems and mathematics.

While still an undergraduate student, I joined a graduate research environment focused on Industrial Informatics and Flexible Manufacturing Systems.

Working alongside researchers, Master's students and PhD candidates exposed me early to multidisciplinary engineering projects and complex engineering environments.

---

# Master's Degree and Industrial Simulation

I later completed a Master's degree in Industrial Informatics and Flexible Manufacturing Systems.

My research focused on control aspects of manufacturing systems while continuing development of a large industrial simulation platform combining manufacturing algorithms, modeling and advanced computer graphics.

This work provided early exposure to simulation, systems modeling and large-scale software engineering.

---

# VOXEL Engenharia de Sistemas

After graduate school, on 1996, I co-founded VOXEL Engenharia de Sistemas.

For nearly twenty years we solved engineering problems across multiple industries.

We were not selling a product. We were selling engineering capability.

Software, hardware, embedded systems, automation, telecommunications, image processing and whatever was necessary to solve the customer's problem.

At the same time I spent more than fifteen years teaching undergraduate engineering and computing courses.

---

# Telecommunications

Telecommunications eventually became one of VOXEL's largest activities.

We developed systems supporting mobile operators and inter-operator settlement processes.

These systems processed tens or hundreds of millions of records per day, years before concepts such as Big Data and cloud computing became common.

Our engineers regularly worked with operators across the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific in deployments, support, customization and training activities.

The environment was intense, requiring rapid response, international travel and support for mission-critical systems.

---

# UAVs and the UAV Outback Challenge

Aviation and model aircraft were lifelong passions.

Around 2009 I became involved with early open UAV autopilot platforms and on 2011 assembled a small team to participate in the UAV Outback Challenge Search and Rescue competition.

We designed an aircraft specifically for the mission, modified ArduPilot and MAVLink behavior, implemented redundancy strategies, safety mechanisms and mission-specific logic.

Although we lost the aircraft shortly before the competition, it remains one of the most intense multidisciplinary engineering efforts of my career.

---

# Recent Years – XMobots

More recently I worked at XMobots.

Initially my work focused on sensors and embedded systems, including thermal cameras, military payloads, agricultural sensors and airborne systems.

Over time I became involved in a much broader range of technical challenges including communications, firmware, integration, propulsion investigations, testing infrastructure, supplier evaluation and operational analysis.

What I enjoyed most was working on problems that crossed disciplinary boundaries, understanding how different subsystems interacted, identifying risks early and helping transform complex requirements into practical engineering solutions.

---

# What Connects Everything

When I look back at my career, I see a consistent pattern:

Understanding complex systems.

Integrating technologies.

Working across disciplines.

Solving difficult real-world problems.

My specialization has always been systems.

---

# My Engineering Perspective on AI

Although I am not primarily a machine learning researcher, I have spent my career building and integrating complex systems involving software, hardware, communications, sensors and autonomy.

What interests me most about AI is how it becomes part of larger operational systems and helps solve real engineering problems.

My strongest contribution is often understanding complete systems, recognizing interactions between subsystems, anticipating problems before they emerge and helping ensure reliable, safe and mission-effective operation.

---

# Why xAI

What attracted me was not only AI itself.

I was thinking about the broader intersection of AI, autonomy, robotics and real-world systems.

Throughout my career I have repeatedly worked on problems where the boundaries between disciplines disappear.

My strength is not necessarily expertise in a single technology.

It is understanding complete systems, connecting disciplines, translating requirements into practical solutions and helping transform technology into operational capability.

I have learned throughout my career that my greatest contribution is usually not expertise in a single component, but rather the ability to understand complete systems, connect different domains, anticipate problems, evaluate alternatives, help teams navigate technical complexity and transform requirements into practical solutions.

Those are the environments where I have contributed the most throughout my career and where I believe I can continue to create the greatest value.

That is what I hope to bring to xAI.
